\section{Conclusion}

Quasars serve as a zero-parallax reference. A reproducible diagnostic framework separates field amplitude, concentration, and leverage in sky-structured Gaia DR3 parallax fields. The framework characterizes a moderately concentrated, scale-persistent residual field with low leverage to small-$K$ masking. The diagnostics quantify whether a residual field exhibits spatial clustering beyond footprint geometry alone and whether dominance by a few sky regions or broad distribution at chosen resolution occurs. Correlation with observation-history proxies indicates the residual field is most naturally explained by calibration-related effects, though specific mechanism attribution (e.g., chromaticity vs. basic-angle variation) requires dedicated calibration modeling beyond this diagnostic framework. The tooling quantifies the amount of map mattering for downstream analyses.

The conclusion concerns masking of a small sky fraction at the tested angular scale; fine-grained or scan-conditioned masking strategies are not addressed.
