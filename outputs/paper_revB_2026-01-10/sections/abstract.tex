A reproducible diagnostic workflow characterizes sky-structured parallax residuals in Gaia DR3 using quasars as an approximate zero-parallax reference population. The QSO parallax field functions as a reference-frame residual surface whose spatial topology may reflect calibration error, contamination, or other unmodeled effects. Metrics quantify that topology along two orthogonal axes: (i) concentration of weighted contribution across the sky via a Lorenz-style curve and Top-20\% contribution statistic, and (ii) leverage of extreme sky regions via a diagnostic top-$K$ masking stress test. Locked dataset snapshot, fixed quality cuts, and pre-specified statistics yield a moderately concentrated but scale-persistent field (Top-20\% contribution $\approx 0.60$ across NSIDE 4--32, exceeding footprint-only null of $\approx 0.34$) with low leverage to small-$K$ masking. Diagnostics quantify spatial behavior under aggregation and masking without identifying physical origin.
